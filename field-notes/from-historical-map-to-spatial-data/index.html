From historical map to spatial data: documenting the difficult middle

The difficult middle

A historical map does not become data simply because it has been scanned. Between image acquisition and analysis lies a chain of choices: source assessment, control-point selection, transformation, uncertainty recording, feature interpretation and validation.

These decisions are often compressed into a single word—georeferencing—yet they determine what later analysis can reasonably claim.

A pipeline as an argument

A reproducible pipeline is not only a technical convenience. It makes the research argument inspectable. Each stage should record its inputs, outputs, assumptions and known limitations.

For colonial-era maps, positional accuracy may vary across the sheet. Features may be symbolic rather than measured. Place names may change, disappear or carry contested histories. The data model should preserve this uncertainty rather than hiding it behind a clean line.

What I am building

The Colonial Map Pipeline separates ingestion, georeferencing, route extraction, spatial analysis and simulation into documented stages. The present focus is Kolkata, but the structure is intended to support comparative work as the evidence base develops.
