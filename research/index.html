Research

Spatial evidence for collective urban life.

Projects combine architecture, spatial analysis, historical inquiry and computational methods to understand how cities organise—and are reorganised by—movement.

Doctoral research · Ongoing

Processional routes and the inherited city

This research investigates how inherited urban structures influence processional movement, spatial culture and collective behaviour in post-colonial cities. Kolkata provides the principal field through which historical maps, route evidence and contemporary spatial analysis are brought together.

Spatial carrying capacity of Durga Puja pandals

A spatially explicit framework for examining capacity around temporary festival architecture. Rather than reducing capacity to floor area, the work connects approach routes, thresholds, circulation, street configuration and temporal crowd conditions.

Colonial map pipeline

A staged workflow for acquiring, standardising, georeferencing and analysing historical city maps. The pipeline records uncertainty and provenance while preparing spatial evidence for route extraction, network analysis and comparative research.

Pedestrian movement and spatial intelligibility

An ongoing inquiry into how spatial configuration, cognition and accessibility influence everyday and exceptional patterns of pedestrian movement, with particular attention to public space and sustainable mobility.
